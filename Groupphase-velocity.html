Mathematical Framework & Physical Interpretation

A quantum particle in motion is described not by a single wave, but by a wave packet—a superposition of plane waves with wave numbers (k) centered around k₀.

  • Phase Velocity (vₚ = ω/k): The speed at which individual crests of the carrier wave propagate. For a free quantum particle (V=0), vₚ = p/(2m).
  • Group Velocity (v_g = dω/dk): The speed at which the overall envelope (the wave packet) propagates. This corresponds to the classical particle velocity (v = p/m).

The Quantum Anomaly: In a dispersive medium like the quantum vacuum for massive particles, the dispersion relation is non-linear: ω(k) = \hbar k² / (2m). Taking the derivative reveals that v_g = 2 × vₚ.

Observe the simulation carefully: The blue carrier waves appear to be born at the front of the red envelope, travel backward relative to the envelope, and disappear at the back! The particle itself moves with the red envelope. Furthermore, due to the non-linear dispersion relation (d²ω/dk² ≠ 0), you will observe the wave packet slowly spreading out (delocalizing) over time.
