Quantum Theory

Quantum Tunneling allows particles to pass through a potential barrier even when their kinetic energy E is less than the barrier height V₀, a phenomenon impossible in classical physics.

The time-independent Schrödinger Equation:

- (ℏ²/2m) (d²ψ/dx²) + V(x)ψ = Eψ

Inside the barrier (E < V₀), the wavefunction does not drop to zero immediately but decays exponentially: ψ(x) ~ e-κx, where κ = √(2m(V₀-E))/ℏ.

Transfer Matrix Method (TMM): For multiple barriers, the wavefunction is modeled piecewise across regions. By enforcing continuity of ψ and dψ/dx at every boundary, we construct a propagation matrix to compute the global Transmission (T) and Reflection (R) coefficients.

Resonant Tunneling: In multi-barrier systems, quantum interference between reflected waves creates discrete energy levels where transmission jumps to 100% (T=1), appearing as sharp peaks in the Energy Scan.
