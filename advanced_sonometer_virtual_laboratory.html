Experiment: The Sonometer

Advanced Virtual Laboratory for determining the frequency of AC mains / Tuning Fork and investigating the laws of transverse vibrations in stretched strings.

Difficulty: Advanced Time: ~45 Mins

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Understand the working principle of a sonometer.
  • Investigate the relationship between frequency, length, and tension.
  • Determine the linear mass density of a wire.
  • Determine the frequency of an AC supply using electromagnetism.
  • Perform comprehensive error analysis.

⚙️ Core Phenomena

Resonance: Occurs when the frequency of the external driving force exactly matches the natural vibrating frequency of the string system. Characterized by maximum amplitude.

Standing Waves: Formed by the superposition of two transverse progressive waves of equal amplitude and frequency traveling in opposite directions.

Theory & Mathematical Modeling

A stretched string clamped at both ends can support transverse stationary waves. When the string is plucked or excited externally, waves travel along its length, reflect at the rigid boundaries (bridges), and interfere to produce standing wave patterns consisting of nodes (points of zero displacement) and antinodes (points of maximum displacement).

1. Fundamental Frequency

For a string of length L, tension T, and mass per unit length (linear mass density) μ, the fundamental frequency f is given by:

f = 12L Tμ

2. Linear Mass Density (μ)

If the string is uniform with a circular cross-section of radius r and is made of a material with density ρ, the linear mass density is:

μ = ml = π r2 ρ

3. Harmonics

The string can vibrate in multiple segments (harmonics). The frequency of the n-th harmonic is:

fn = n2L Tμ = n f1

4. AC Sonometer Mode

If a non-magnetic conducting wire carries alternating current of frequency fAC in a constant magnetic field (or if a magnetic wire is driven by an AC electromagnet), the driving force frequency is fdrive = 2fAC (for electromagnet) or fAC (for AC carrying wire in uniform B-field). Resonance happens when fdrive ≈ fn.

Dimensional Analysis Validation

[T] = M L T-2

[μ] = M L-1

[T/μ] = (M L T-2) / (M L-1) = L2 T-2

√(T/μ) = L T-1 (Velocity)

[f] = [1/L] × [Velocity] = L-1 × L T-1 = T-1 = Hertz (Hz)

Interactive Apparatus Overview

Standard Laboratory Procedure

  1. Place the sonometer on a stable, horizontal table. Ensure the pulley is completely frictionless.
  2. Stretch the wire over the bridges and pass it over the pulley. Suspend a suitable mass (e.g., 2 kg) from the hanger.
  3. Select a tuning fork of known frequency (e.g., 256 Hz) and strike it gently on a rubber pad.
  4. Place the vibrating tuning fork stem on the sonometer box to transfer vibrations to the string.
  5. Place a small, inverted V-shaped paper rider at the center of the string between the movable bridges A and B.
  6. Gradually change the distance between bridges A and B. Observe the paper rider.
  7. When the natural frequency of the string segment exactly matches the tuning fork frequency, resonance occurs. The string vibrates with maximum amplitude, throwing off the paper rider.
  8. Measure the length L between the bridges using the attached meter scale.
  9. Repeat the process to find the mean resonant length. Change the mass/tension to perform Experiment B.

⚠️ Practical Precautions

  • Ensure the wire is uniform and free of kinks.
  • The pulley must be frictionless. Lubricate if necessary in a real lab.
  • Avoid adding excessive mass to prevent the wire from crossing its elastic limit.
  • Place the paper rider exactly in the middle of the vibrating segment.
  • Wait for the vibration to stabilize before declaring resonance.
