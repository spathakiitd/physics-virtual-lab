Theoretical Framework

1. Introduction & Lorentz Force

When a current-carrying conductor or semiconductor is placed in a transverse magnetic field, a potential difference is generated in a direction perpendicular to both the current and the magnetic field. This is the Hall Effect.

The magnetic force on a charge $q$ moving with drift velocity $\mathbf{v}_d$ is given by the Lorentz force:

$$ \mathbf{F}_L = q(\mathbf{E} + \mathbf{v}_d \times \mathbf{B}) $$

Charges accumulate on the transverse surfaces until the generated Hall electric field $\mathbf{E}_H$ perfectly balances the magnetic force: $qE_H = qv_dB$.

2. Hall Voltage & Hall Coefficient

Since current density $J = nqv_d$ and $V_H = E_H w$ (where $w$ is width), substituting current $I = Jwt$ gives the fundamental Hall voltage equation:

$$ V_H = \frac{R_H \cdot I_H \cdot B}{t} $$

Where $t$ is thickness and $R_H$ is the Hall Coefficient, defined for a single dominant carrier as:

$$ R_H = \frac{1}{nq} $$

Note: In real semiconductors, a Hall factor $r_H$ is often included ($R_H = r_H / nq$) due to scattering mechanisms. For this lab, we assume $r_H \approx 1$.

3. Carrier Concentration, Type & Mobility
  • Type: The sign of $V_H$ (and thus $R_H$) determines carrier type. $R_H < 0$ implies electrons (n-type). $R_H > 0$ implies holes (p-type).
  • Concentration ($n$): $n = \frac{1}{e |R_H|}$
  • Mobility ($\mu$): Related to conductivity $\sigma = ne\mu = \frac{1}{\rho}$. Thus, $\mu = |R_H| \sigma$.

Experimental Procedure

Part A: Setup & Calibration

  1. Go to the Simulation tab.
  2. Select your desired semiconductor material from the control panel.
  3. Set the sample thickness $t$.
  4. Observe the Gauss meter. Vary Solenoid Current ($I_s$) to establish a Magnetic Field ($B$).

Part B: Data Collection

  1. Apply a Constant Hall Current ($I_H$).
  2. Note the generated Hall Voltage ($V_H$) on the Millivoltmeter.
  3. Click "Add Reading" to record the data in the Observations table.
  4. Vary $I_H$ keeping $B$ constant, and take multiple readings.
  5. Vary $B$ keeping $I_H$ constant, and take multiple readings.
  6. Use the Reverse Polarity buttons to eliminate offset errors (record both + and - states).

Part C: Analysis

  1. Navigate to the Analysis tab.
  2. Observe the generated $V_H$ vs $I_H$ and $V_H$ vs $B$ graphs.
  3. Use the "Compute Physics Parameters" engine to perform linear regression.
  4. Determine $R_H$, $n$, type, and $\mu$.
  5. Generate and save your final Report.
