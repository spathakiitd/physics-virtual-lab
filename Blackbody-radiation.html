The Physics of Blackbody Radiation

What is a Blackbody?

An ideal blackbody absorbs all incident electromagnetic radiation, regardless of frequency or angle of incidence. To stay in thermal equilibrium, it must emit radiation at the same rate it absorbs it. The spectrum of this emitted radiation depends only on the body's temperature, not its composition.

The Ultraviolet Catastrophe (Classical Failure)

Classical physics attempted to model this using the equipartition theorem, leading to the Rayleigh-Jeans Law:

B(λ, T) = 2ckBT / λ4

While accurate at long wavelengths (infrared/radio), this formula predicts that emitted energy approaches infinity as wavelength approaches zero (the ultraviolet region). This physical impossibility was dubbed the "Ultraviolet Catastrophe."

Planck's Quantum Solution

In 1900, Max Planck solved this by proposing a radical hypothesis: electromagnetic energy can only be emitted or absorbed in discrete "quanta" (packets) of energy, proportional to the frequency (E = hν). This led to Planck's Law:

B(λ, T) = (2hc² / λ⁵) * [ 1 / (ehc/λkBT - 1) ]

This perfectly matched experimental data at all wavelengths, avoiding the catastrophe and giving birth to Quantum Mechanics.

Other Important Laws

  • Wien's Displacement Law: The peak wavelength is inversely proportional to temperature (λmax = b/T). As an object gets hotter, its peak emission shifts to shorter, bluer wavelengths.
  • Stefan-Boltzmann Law: The total energy radiated per unit surface area is proportional to the fourth power of temperature (j* = σT⁴). Doubling the temperature increases power output by 16 times!
  • Wien's Approximation: A historically important approximation that works well at short wavelengths but fails at long ones: B(λ, T) ≈ (2hc² / λ⁵) * e-hc/λkBT

Domain Insights

Notice that the peak of the Planck curve shifts when switching from Wavelength to Frequency domain. This is not an error! It arises due to the non-linear transformation between λ and ν (dν = -c/λ² dλ). The physics (total area/energy) remains identical.
